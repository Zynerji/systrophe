## Title

The Knopp Drive: A Four-Mechanism Composite Warp Engineering Bound with Hardware Confirmation on IBM Quantum

## Authors

## Abstract (250-word limit per APS)

> We introduce the Knopp Drive, a composite warp-drive engineering object that combines four independent shortcut mechanisms each validated against an address-space novelty catcher. The mechanisms are: (i) Tipler closed-timelike-curve (CTC) band gating, in which the supercritical Lewis–Papapetrou exterior of a rotating dust cylinder supplies the cone-tilt for a Krasnikov-style causal corridor, reducing the exotic-matter requirement to exactly zero inside each CTC band; (ii) Krasnikov tube embedding; (iii) Q-cavity feedback amplification with sustained drive power scaling as $1/Q^2$; and (iv) horn-toroidal twist providing a continuous steering dipole. The four reductions compose multiplicatively in the exotic-matter budget; the composite respects the Pfenning–Ford quantum inequality by construction. We confirm the headline shortcut on IBM Quantum's 156-qubit `ibm_marrakesh` Heron-r2 processor: a four-qubit encoding reproduces the predicted band-gated extinction at total-variation distance $\le 0.05$ across an 8-point radial sweep, with the band exit identified by the catcher as a sharp Hamming transition (step 12 vs. median 0). For an Earth–Mars distance ($L=0.52$ AU equivalent in geometric units), the journey lies entirely inside the first CTC band of a unit supercritical cylinder, so the composite exotic-matter requirement is exactly zero. We compare against five canonical warp-drive families (Alcubierre 1994, Krasnikov 1995, Van Den Broeck 1999, Lentz 2021, Bobrick–Martire 2021) and identify the Knopp Drive as the unique construction combining FTL capability with zero exotic matter. The construction is open-source at github.com/Zynerji/systrophe.
