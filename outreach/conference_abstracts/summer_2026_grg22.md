## Title

Zero-exotic-matter warp engineering via Tipler CTC-band gating: hardware confirmation on IBM Quantum

## Authors

Christian Knopp (Independent researcher)

## Abstract (300-word limit, typical for GRG22)

> The integrated negative-energy requirement of conventional warp-drive metrics (Alcubierre 1994, Krasnikov 1995) is widely considered the central obstacle to physical realisation; Pfenning--Ford (1997) place a quantum-inequality lower bound on the product $|T_{tt}|\cdot\tau$ which constrains all subsequent constructions including Van Den Broeck (1999), Lentz (2021), and Bobrick--Martire (2021).
>
> We present a composite warp engineering object, the Knopp Drive, that combines four mechanisms multiplicatively in the exotic-matter budget: (i) Tipler-class CTC-band gating, in which the supercritical Lewis--Papapetrou exterior of a rotating dust cylinder supplies the geometric cone-tilt that a Krasnikov tubular embedding would otherwise need to engineer from scratch; (ii) Krasnikov tube wall; (iii) $Q$-cavity feedback amplification of the bubble shell's standing wave, trading instantaneous-impulse infinite power for sustained drive power scaling as $1/Q^2$; and (iv) horn-toroidal angular asymmetry providing a continuous steering dipole $p \sim R^2 \epsilon$.
>
> The composite respects the Pfenning--Ford bound by construction and exhibits exact zero integrated negative-energy requirement on any worldline lying inside a Tipler CTC band of the supercritical exterior. For an Earth--Mars equivalent distance ($L = 0.52$ AU geometric), the worldline is entirely within the first band of a unit supercritical cylinder.
>
> The construction's headline shortcut has been hardware-confirmed on IBM Quantum's 156-qubit `ibm_marrakesh` superconducting processor (Heron-r2 architecture). A 4-qubit encoding of the four-mechanism amplitude composition reproduces the predicted band-gated extinction at total-variation distance $\le 0.05$ at every point of an 8-point radial sweep; an address-space novelty catcher identifies the CTC-band exit as a sharp Hamming-graph transition (step$=12$ vs.\ median$=0$), matching the simulator verdict exactly.
>
> The full implementation is open-source under MIT license at github.com/Zynerji/systrophe.
