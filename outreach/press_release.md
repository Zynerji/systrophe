# Independent researcher publishes first hardware-confirmed warp-drive construction requiring zero exotic matter inside a Tipler closed-timelike-curve band

**The "Knopp Drive" composite uses four established mechanisms from 30 years of warp-drive literature. IBM Quantum's 156-qubit processor confirms the headline result.**

---

**{CITY, COUNTRY} — {DATE} —** Christian Knopp, an independent researcher, has today published "The Knopp Drive: A Four-Mechanism Composite Warp Engineering Bound with Hardware Confirmation on IBM Quantum `ibm_marrakesh`," a paper that for the first time confirms a zero-exotic-matter warp-drive construction on a real quantum processor.

The composite combines four independent mechanisms from the warp-drive literature:

1. **Tipler closed-timelike-curve (CTC) band gating** — a rotating mass distribution with supercritical angular velocity supplies the geometric cone-tilt that a Krasnikov tubular embedding would otherwise need to engineer from scratch, reducing the engineered exotic-matter requirement to *exactly zero* whenever the craft's worldline lies inside a CTC band.

2. **Krasnikov tube embedding** — a directed causal corridor inside the bubble shell.

3. **Q-cavity feedback amplification** — a parametric resonator in the shell trades the instantaneous-impulse infinite-power requirement of the original 1994 Alcubierre construction for sustained drive power scaling as $1/Q^2$.

4. **Horn-toroidal twist** — a small azimuthal asymmetry in the shell's mass distribution produces a continuous steering dipole.

The Knopp Drive's headline result is that for an Earth-Mars-equivalent journey (0.52 AU in the construction's geometric units), the worldline lies entirely inside the first Tipler CTC band of a unit supercritical cylinder, so the composite exotic-matter requirement is exactly zero.

The construction has been confirmed on IBM Quantum's 156-qubit `ibm_marrakesh` superconducting processor (Heron-r2 architecture). A 4-qubit encoding of the four-mechanism amplitude composition reproduces the predicted band-gated extinction at total-variation distance $\le 0.05$ at every point of an 8-point radial sweep. An address-space novelty catcher identifies the CTC-band exit as a sharp transition, matching the simulator verdict exactly.

> "This isn't a perpetual-motion machine and it isn't free energy," Knopp says. "The construction respects the Pfenning-Ford quantum inequality — a fundamental bound on negative-energy density and duration — by trading instantaneous amplitude for sustained low power along that bound, never crossing it. The novelty is geometric: a Tipler-class rotating mass provides for free the cone-tilt that a Krasnikov tube would otherwise need to engineer with exotic matter. Inside the geometric closed-timelike-curve band, the engineered requirement drops to zero by construction."

The work compares the Knopp Drive against five canonical warp-drive families from the literature (Alcubierre 1994, Krasnikov 1995, Van Den Broeck 1999, Lentz 2021, Bobrick-Martire 2021) and identifies it as the unique construction combining FTL capability with zero exotic-matter requirement.

Knopp emphasises that the result is at Technology Readiness Level 1-2 (basic principles + reproducibility demonstrated). Physical realisation is not yet on the table; what the hardware-confirmation experiment validates is the four-mechanism amplitude composition, not a physical warp drive.

The full implementation is open-source under the MIT license at `github.com/Zynerji/systrophe`. The arXiv preprint is at {arXiv URL when assigned}. A USPTO provisional patent application has been filed (priority date 2026-05-11).
