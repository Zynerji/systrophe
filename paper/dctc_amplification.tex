\title{\bfseries Clifford-structured Deutsch-CTC channels:\\
Empirical amplification, spectral oracle,\\
and the encoding-dependence of chronology protection}
\author{Christian Knopp\\\texttt{cknopp@gmail.com}}
\date{2026-05-11}

\begin{abstract}
We report a sustained numerical investigation of Deutsch's fixed-point
formulation of closed timelike curves (D-CTC, \cite{Deutsch1991}) on
small-Hilbert-space channels. Across 19 phases (about 30{,}000
sampled channels) we establish three empirical regularities that are
not, to our knowledge, in the prior literature: (i) the iteration
count to convergence is log-normally distributed with Pearson
correlation $r = 0.99$ to the second-largest channel-superoperator
eigenvalue, giving an $\mathcal{O}(d^6)$ spectral oracle for
convergence prediction; (ii) the channel class that produces
near-pure fixed points and the Aaronson--Watrous \cite{AaronsonWatrous2009}
state-distinguisher amplification is \emph{not} generic Haar
randomness but a Clifford-like subgroup (permutation $\times$
diagonal-of-fourth-roots), at rate $\sim$100$\times$ above Haar; and
(iii) the empirical amplification persists at the chronology-
protected SystrophePair configuration $\delta = \pi$ for abstract
channel encodings but is suppressed for channel constructions that
literally encode the local CTC tensor structure. These findings
sharpen the Aaronson--Watrous result by making explicit its
contingency on channel implementation: the polynomial-time PSPACE
speedup is available in post-selection / Clifford-circuit
implementations even at chronology-protected configurations, but is
killed by chronology protection when the channel structure directly
inherits spacetime CTC content. All code, data, and reproduction
scripts are at \texttt{github.com/Zynerji/systrophe}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Deutsch's 1991 formulation of closed-timelike-curve quantum mechanics
\cite{Deutsch1991} avoids paradoxes by requiring a CPTP channel
$\mathcal{E}: \rho \mapsto \mathrm{Tr}_{\text{CR}}[U(\sigma_{\text{CR}} \otimes \rho)U^\dagger]$
to admit a self-consistent fixed point. Aaronson and
Watrous \cite{AaronsonWatrous2009} showed that a polynomial-time
computation with access to such fixed-point oracles solves
PSPACE-complete problems, including distinguishing arbitrarily close
mixed states better than the Helstrom bound \cite{Helstrom1976} on
ordinary quantum measurement permits.

Two separate questions arise empirically:

\begin{itemize}
\item For \emph{which} unitaries $U$ does the D-CTC iteration yield
useful structure (high-purity fixed points, distinguishability
amplification)?
\item Does the amplification survive in the regime where the
chronology-protection conjecture \cite{Hawking1992} predicts
suppression of the underlying classical CTCs?
\end{itemize}

We present numerical answers to both.

\section{Setup and definitions}

\subsection{D-CTC iteration}

Given a unitary $U$ on a joint Hilbert space
$\mathcal{H}_{\text{CR}} \otimes \mathcal{H}_{\text{CTC}}$ of dimensions
$d_{\text{CR}} \times d_{\text{CTC}}$ and a chronology-respecting input
state $\sigma_{\text{CR}}$, the D-CTC fixed-point equation is
\begin{equation}
\rho^* = \mathrm{Tr}_{\text{CR}}\!\big[ U(\sigma_{\text{CR}} \otimes \rho^*) U^\dagger \big].
\label{eq:dctc-fp}
\end{equation}
We solve (\ref{eq:dctc-fp}) by iteration
$\rho_{n+1} = \mathcal{E}(\rho_n)$ starting from random pure states
$\rho_0$, with tolerance $\|\rho_{n+1} - \rho_n\|_F < 10^{-10}$.

\subsection{Channel superoperator}

Writing $\mathcal{E}$ as a $d_{\text{CTC}}^2 \times d_{\text{CTC}}^2$
matrix $\mathbf{E}$ in column-stacked basis, the iteration's
asymptotic convergence rate is controlled by $|\lambda_2(\mathbf{E})|$,
the second-largest-magnitude eigenvalue. The largest, $|\lambda_1| = 1$
by CPTP.

\subsection{State-distinguisher amplification}

For two close mixed states $\sigma_a, \sigma_b$ on
$\mathcal{H}_{\text{CR}}$ with trace distance
$\|\sigma_a - \sigma_b\|_1/2 = \epsilon$, the Helstrom success rate
on the inputs is $\tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{\epsilon}{2}$. The D-CTC
\emph{amplification factor} is
\begin{equation}
A = \frac{\|\rho_a^* - \rho_b^*\|_1}{\|\sigma_a - \sigma_b\|_1},
\end{equation}
where $\rho_{a,b}^*$ are the fixed points conditioned on $\sigma_a$
and $\sigma_b$. The Aaronson--Watrous result predicts $A \gg 1$ for
suitable $U$, enabling polynomial-time PSPACE computation.

\section{Numerical findings}

We focus on $d_{\text{CR}} = 2$, $d_{\text{CTC}} = 3$ unless noted.
All sample sizes are $\ge 1000$ unitaries per ensemble.

\subsection{Spectral oracle for convergence rate}

Over 2000 Haar-random unitaries $U$, the empirical iteration count
$N_\epsilon$ to converge to tolerance $\epsilon$ correlates with
$|\lambda_2|$ via the standard mixing-time prediction
\begin{equation}
N_\epsilon^{\text{predicted}} = -\log\epsilon \big/ \log|\lambda_2|^{-1},
\end{equation}
with Pearson $r = 0.99$ and log-log slope $0.92$ (theory: $1.0$).
The iteration-count distribution is itself log-normal (AIC advantage
$\Delta = 2101$ over an exponential fit), indicative of a
multiplicative-cascade structure in the iteration dynamics.

\subsection{Power-law dim-dependence}

The median iteration count scales as
$\langle N \rangle \propto d_{\text{CR}}^{-\alpha}$ with $\alpha = 0.85$
for $d_{\text{CR}} \in \{2, ..., 8\}$, $d_{\text{CTC}} \in \{3, 4, 5\}$.
The exponent is essentially flat across $d_{\text{CTC}}$.

\subsection{Clifford structure produces high-purity fixed points}

Across $d_{\text{CR}} = 2$ ensembles at $d_{\text{CTC}} = 3$, the
fraction of Haar-random unitaries producing fixed points with purity
$> 0.9$ is approximately $0.4\%$. Distance-to-separable and other
generic structural measures do not discriminate the high-purity
subclass (Pearson $r < 0.1$).

By contrast, the subgroup of unitaries of the form
$U = P \cdot \mathrm{diag}(\pm 1, \pm i, ...)$ where $P$ is a
permutation matrix --- a strict subgroup of the Clifford group ---
produces purity $> 0.9$ in $\approx 40\%$ of samples, a
$100\times$ rate enhancement (Table~\ref{tab:clifford-rate}).

\subsection{Aaronson--Watrous amplification at close inputs}

For two close mixed states with trace distance $\epsilon$, the
amplification observed for the best Clifford-like channel grows
sharply as $\epsilon$ shrinks:

For inputs at $\epsilon = 0.05$ where Helstrom barely exceeds
guessing ($52.5\%$), the optimised Clifford-like D-CTC channel
discriminates at $83.3\%$ --- a $13.3\times$ enhancement of the
advantage-over-guessing. The amplification is empirically the
Aaronson--Watrous signature \cite{AaronsonWatrous2009}.

The amplification is robust to imperfect implementation: a
depolarising noise of $10\%$ per iteration step reduces the
speedup at $\epsilon = 0.10$ from $1.82$ to $1.52$ (still
significant).

\subsection{Encoding dependence vs.\ chronology protection}

A natural physics question: is the amplification suppressed at
chronology-protected configurations? Using the Systrophe Tipler-pair
formalism \cite{Knopp2026Systrophe} where the pair offset
$\delta = \pi$ extinguishes the classical CTC content (the
\emph{phasor extinction theorem}), we test thirteen $U(\delta)$
constructions:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Channel-construction taxonomy: D-CTC amplification at
$\delta = \pi$ vs.\ physical CTC content.}
\label{tab:encoding-taxonomy}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Encoding regime & Examples & Amp.\ at $\delta = \pi$ \\
\midrule
\textbf{Abstract} & Clifford, Floquet, generic Hamiltonian & varies; often peak \\
\textbf{Direct CTC} & $U_{ij} \sim L_{\text{pair}}(r_{ij})$ literally & $0$ (collapses) \\
\textbf{LP-Hamiltonian} & $H \sim (F, K, L)$ on Clifford backbone & $0.18$ (constant) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

In the abstract regime, the amplification is decoupled from the
spacetime CTC content (mean Pearson $-0.02$ across 4 distinct
constructions). In the direct-CTC regime, the channel matrix
collapses when $L_{\text{pair}}$ vanishes, killing amplification by
structural degeneracy. In the LP-Hamiltonian regime, the
amplification is muted but constant across the regime
($\omega R = 0.6, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0$).

The empirical conclusion: the Aaronson--Watrous speedup is
\emph{contingent on channel implementation}, not on physical
CTC existence.

\section{Implications}

The trichotomy of Table~\ref{tab:encoding-taxonomy} clarifies what
chronology protection implies for D-CTC computation:

\begin{itemize}
\item Abstract D-CTC implementations (post-selection circuits
\cite{Aaronson2005}, pre-engineered Clifford gates) achieve
Aaronson--Watrous-style amplification regardless of whether the
underlying spacetime supports classical CTCs.
\item Direct-CTC implementations (channels literally built from the
local CTC tensor) are killed by chronology protection together
with the CTCs themselves.
\item Physically grounded LP-Hamiltonian channels achieve perfect
purity (rank-1 fixed points) but only modest amplification,
independent of the $(\omega, R)$ regime.
\end{itemize}

This refines the standard reading of \cite{AaronsonWatrous2009}: the
PSPACE-solving capability is a property of the \emph{channel}, not
of the spacetime. In a universe where Hawking's chronology-
protection conjecture is empirically true, the speedup remains
accessible via post-selection-based implementations.

\section{Computational implications and open questions}

The Clifford-structured high-purity D-CTC class can be efficiently
sampled (just permutations and fourth-root diagonals), making it
implementable in any quantum simulator with classical post-selection.
The empirical $13.3\times$ amplification at $\epsilon = 0.05$ inputs
suggests the AW algorithm is testable on existing NISQ hardware with
modest classical post-processing.

Open questions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Does the amplification scale with $d_{\text{CR}}$ beyond
$d_{\text{CR}} = 2$? Our data shows the high-purity rate vanishes
sharply for $d_{\text{CR}} \ge 3$; whether the AW amplification
persists at larger $d_{\text{CR}}$ requires further study.
\item The trichotomy of channel encodings is sharp empirically;
is there a theorem connecting it to the structure of the channel's
Stinespring dilation?
\item The log-normal iteration-count distribution suggests a
multiplicative-cascade microscopic mechanism. What is the
underlying random process?
\end{enumerate}
